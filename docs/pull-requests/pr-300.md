# [PR 300](https://github.com/vig-os/scitadel/pull/300) feat(acquire): candidate kinds — a citation page is not a reading route

#298. The root cause is not the ranker's comparison any more — it's that a candidate's URL was never classified, so a **bibliographic record** could wear a publication's version word and win on version dominance.

Measured live, `GET api.openalex.org/works/doi:10.1101/2025.06.14.659707`:

```
best_oa_location: doi:10.1101/2025.06.14.659707   acceptedVersion
locations[1] id=pmid:40667369                     publishedVersion
  landing_page_url = pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40667369     pdf_url = null
locations[2] id=pmh:oai:pubmedcentral.nih.gov:12262699    submittedVersion
```

Rank 1 was the PubMed page and version strictly dominates — so the bioRxiv transform's own `preprint` candidate could never overtake it.

## The `landing_page_url` rule is not enough

A location's role in the *fields* is `pdf_url` vs `landing_page_url`, and the PubMed URL arrives in **`landing_page_url`** — classified `LandingPage`, kept, ranked. That rule alone fixes nothing.

The field that actually separates it from the PMC copy next door is OpenAlex's **typed identifier prefix**: `pmid:` vs `pmh:oai:pubmedcentral.nih.gov:` vs `doi:`. That's the source naming its own role, which is the only honest basis for classification — a host list is a reliability heuristic with a typing hat, and `best_oa_location` here is `doi.org`, so *that* prefix decides first.

`openalex_location_kind` reads `pdf_url`, then `id` prefix, then `landing_page_url`, in that order.

## What is excluded, and at what cost

`CitationRecord` and `Manifest` are not candidates. **`LandingPage` stays** — ADR-007 §3 step 6 *is* the publisher landing page, and over-excluding is the mistake that cost `similarity-checking` a working route.

`Unknown` is not set-dressing for `LandingPage` — "no role stated" keeps the source's version word, which leaves a Crossref `link[]` with no `content-type` byte-identical to before.

`Manifest` is **named and has no producer**, pinned by `the_manifest_kind_is_named_and_empty` (naming a known-empty state so it cannot be silently filled — no kind has a producer at present). Nothing in the metadata pass *can* know pre-fetch that `doi.org/10.18434/*` answers with DataCite JSON rather than a document.

## One version-withholding rule, narrowed against measurement

#298 proposed a `LandingPage` must not be raised to `VoR` by an identity source. Implemented broadly, it **regresses a working copy**: `10.1093/neuonc/noz175.233`'s `best_oa_location` is the PMC article page filed `publishedVersion`, it serves 94 KB of article HTML, so the word *is* a claim about those bytes — the broad rule drops a working VoR to `Unstated` and the fetch lands on the resolver's page, which 403s.

So the rule is `LandingPage` **and the URL is the DOI resolver** — the one shape the pass can name that serves no document by construction. Pinned by `a_doi_resolver_landing_page_takes_the_work_version_not_its_location_word`.

## A kind-vs-fetch-step conflict, resolved and named

§3 step 3 reads "OA repository locations **from OpenAlex and Unpaywall**" — the route names the step, so the same kind sits on two rungs. Moving all `LandingPage`s to step 6 costs `neuonc` that working PMC article HTML. So only `DataCite`'s landing page moves to step 6 (§3 has no rung for it at all, the gap #292 reported); OpenAlex and Unpaywall landing pages stay at 3, pinned by `a_landing_page_from_a_route_that_cannot_say_is_step_six`.

**`Rank` gained no field and its axes are untouched** — version strictly dominates licence, still pinned by the 128-cell ordering test from #299. What changed is the *data* on two `Candidate`s, not the comparator.

## What this does not do

**Clause 1 of #260 is still not green.** On the three probe DOIs: rank 1 is a PubMed page **0/3** (was 2/3); a bioRxiv PDF is rank 1 **1/3** (was 0/3).

The other two now resolve to *better* documents than the bioRxiv transform would have served — `10.1101/2024.11.19.624167` to PMC's own author-manuscript PDF, and `10.1101/2025.06.14.659707` to Europe PMC's CC-BY author manuscript at §3 **step 1**. `am > preprint` is the settled rule and step 1 is the ADR's own order, so both are correct. Reaching 3/3 would need over-excluding two working OA sources or inverting `am > preprint`.

**The residual is an ADR-007 §3 precedence question, not a bug**: §3's fetch order makes Europe PMC's author manuscript outrank the preprint server's copy, while #260 asks for the preprint copy. Two different stated goals. It needs an ADR decision, and one is proposed below.

Also honest: two rows lost "obtained" bytes, because what they held was a PubMed abstract page (`10.1093/clinchem`, `10.1039/d5cc03932k`). Both now report the publisher's 403 instead, which is the truth.

## Proposed ADR-007 §3 amendment

§3 step 1 should say the author-manuscript rung is for **works whose version of record is not open**, rather than as a general preference. A work that has its own OA VoR at a preprint server is better served by the preprint copy than by a manuscript; a work with no OA VoR is best served by the manuscript. That is a precedence decision, which is why it is not made here.
